## 4. The Cross-Dataset and Leakage Drop, Stated Plainly

Under earlier un-grouped 80/20 random splits, published claims in `README.md` exhibited severe evaluation data leakage:

1. **Job Scam Text (DIFrauD)**:
   - **Claimed Score:** F1-macro `0.8346`.
   - **Honest MEASURED Score:** F1 `0.5931` (95% CI: `[0.4897, 0.6809]`).
   - **Plain Truth:** Scammers produce hundreds of identical job postings differing only by company name, salary number, or recruiter name. An un-grouped split places identical scam template twins into both train and test sets. The model simply memorized template wording rather than general scam semantics. When near-duplicate grouping ($\ge 0.90$ cosine similarity) is enforced, F1 drops by **0.2415 points**, revealing true generalization against novel scam templates.
2. **Sentinel Multiclass Fraud**:
   - **Claimed Score:** F1-macro `0.9837`.
   - **Honest MEASURED Score:** Macro-F1 `0.9307` across 4 categories.
   - **Plain Truth:** The raw dataset contains 60% scams, giving an illusion of high precision in-distribution. When evaluated under a realistic 5% base rate, binary fraud precision collapses from 0.88 down to **`0.1972`**, generating 214 false alarms for every 1,000 legitimate messages.
3. **URL Phishing (CompPhish)**:
   - **Claimed Scores:** Char N-Gram F1 `0.9524`, Tabular HistGB F1 `0.9680`.
   - **Honest MEASURED Scores:** Char N-Gram F1 `0.9287` (`0.9320` calibrated), Tabular HistGB F1 `0.9421` (`0.9443` calibrated).
   - **Plain Truth:** In CompPhish, hundreds of subdomains share apex domains (e.g. 832 URLs under `vercel.app`, 515 under `webflow.io`). Un-grouped splits leak domain representations across folds. Grouping strictly by registered domain closes this loophole. Crucially, at a realistic 5% base rate, precision drops to **~31–36%**, generating ~94–112 false alerts per 1,000 links.

## 5. The Biggest Suspected Artifacts

1. **Hosting Provider N-Gram Memorization (URL Phishing)**:
   - Strongest positive features include `.we`, `app`, `365`, `site`, `ebl`, `bly`, `weeb`, `weebly`, `eebly`.
   - The model learns to penalize the hosting provider (Weebly, Webflow, Vercel) rather than structural phishing mechanics. Legitimate developer portfolios or small businesses hosted on free tiers are falsely flagged.
2. **Institutional & Regulatory Entity Overfitting (Sentinel Multiclass)**:
   - Strongest signals in Government Impersonation: `cbi`, `narcotics`, `interpol`, `trai`, `dcp`, `si`, `arrest`.
   - Strongest signals in Bank Phishing: `pnb`, `hdfc`, `kyc`, `the otp`.
   - Scammers altering agency names to state police or smaller regional cooperative banks evade detection, while legitimate advisory alerts mentioning these agencies trigger false positives.
3. **Recruitment Lexical Collisions (DIFrauD)**:
   - Strongest positive signals: `data entry`, `1000`, `apply using`, `maintenance`, `reps`, `earn`.
   - Legitimate accounting, billing, and back-office postings trigger false alerts due to high concentrations of transaction vocabulary.
